# 06 — Live run: 300 prompts (paid)

Date: 2026-10-03. Spend this round **$1.91**: judge re-check $0.135 and live run $1.775. Total
paid spend to date **$2.12** of a $3 cap.

Models (OpenRouter): small `qwen/qwen3-30b-a3b-instruct-2507`, reference
`anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5`, judge `google/gemini-3.6-flash` (reasoning effort low).
Answers capped at 1,024 tokens. Every request answered by Qwen and checked against Sonnet.
Config: `experiments/12_live_300.yaml`.

## Changes after the 50-prompt pilot

1. **Two verdicts per comparison.** CONTENT (substance, the error rate) and FORMAT (following
   output instructions), reported separately.
2. **Mostly open-ended prompts.** 200 open-ended (grade-school math 60, MBPP code 40, summaries 30,
   MT-Bench 30, abstract→title 20, bias detection 20) + 100 multiple choice.
3. **1,024-token answers**, and answers that hit the limit are flagged.
4. Parallel calls (8 workers): ~10 minutes instead of about an hour.

## Judge re-check (same 50 known pairs as the pilot)

| | old judge (one verdict) | new judge (content + format) |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement with task truth | 64% [49-77%] | **94% [83-99%]** |
| Truly worse → said worse | 24/25 | 24/25 |
| Not worse → said worse | 17/25 | **2/25** |
| Same verdict in both orders | 96% | 100% |

Separating format from content removed nearly all the false alarms.

## Results

| | Qwen worse (content) | Format slips |
|---|---|---|
| **All 300** | **28% [23-33%]** | 9% [6-13%] |
| Open-ended (200) | 33% [27-40%] | 2% |
| Multiple choice (100) | 18% [11-27%] | 23% |

By task (n, content worse): grade-school math 60 → **5%**, MBPP 40 → 40%, summaries 30 → 43%,
MT-Bench 30 → 27%, bias detection 20 → 40%, abstract→title 20 → **90%**.

- Cost: Qwen served everything for $0.021 vs ~$0.95 estimated for Sonnet, **97.8% cheaper**.
  Checking all 300 cost $1.76, so this run's net savings are negative by design. At a 1-3%
  check rate the checking cost would be ~2-5% of the Sonnet bill.
- 64/300 answers identical (no judge call). Judge output parsed 100%, order-consistent 95.7%.
- Hit max_tokens: Qwen 6.3% (19, mostly MT-Bench and code; 8 judged worse), Sonnet 8.0%.

## How much to trust which numbers

- **Objective tasks: trustworthy.** Math at 5% matches what a strong 30B model should do, and
  the judge agreed with known truth 94% of the time.
- **Subjective tasks: judge preference.** On abstract→title, Qwen's titles are as good as
  Sonnet's in the examples checked. Sonnet adds bold formatting and "alternative options", and
  the judge rewards the extra effort. The 90% there, and probably part of the summary and MT-Bench
  numbers, reflects style preference, not errors.
- So the 33% open-ended figure is an upper bound on real quality loss. A content-only judge that
  ignores extras, or hand-labelling a sample of subjective cases, is needed before quoting it.

## What next (if continued)

- Tell the judge to ignore unrequested extras (alternatives, markdown) on subjective tasks, and
  re-judge from cache: only judge calls are paid (~$0.30), since answers are cached.
- Hand-label ~30 subjective pairs to measure judge preference bias directly.
- Report truncation as its own failure type, or raise the cap for long-form tasks.
